## Users

MedStory is for people living with chronic or long-term health conditions who need to remember, organize, understand, and communicate years of medical history. Primary users include people managing conditions such as chronic pelvic pain, chronic prostatitis, IBS, Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis, chronic neurological conditions, autoimmune diseases, oncology care, chronic allergies, and persistent unexplained symptoms.

Secondary users include parents managing a child's medical history, adults managing multiple conditions, people who frequently visit healthcare providers, and anyone with a large volume of medical documentation. Users may be stressed, symptomatic, preparing for a doctor visit, or trying to reconstruct important details from scattered records, photos, prescriptions, reports, and notes.

Their core job is to preserve a clear medical story over time with as little administrative burden as possible.

## Product Purpose

MedStory is a personal medical memory application that transforms scattered medical documents, treatments, symptoms, and health events into a structured, searchable, and understandable health story.

It exists to help users:

- Store medical history in one trusted place.
- Build and review a chronological medical timeline.
- Understand medical terminology and documents in plain language.
- Track symptoms, diagnoses, medications, examinations, procedures, hospitalizations, and treatment outcomes.
- Prepare concise summaries for healthcare professionals.
- Reduce the need to repeatedly reconstruct their history from memory.

MedStory is not a diagnostic tool, not a replacement for healthcare professionals, not a telemedicine platform, and not an AI doctor. Its success is measured by whether users can capture, understand, review, and communicate their medical history with less effort and more confidence.

## Brand Personality

Calm, trustworthy, and clear.

The product should feel like a careful medical organizer that respects the user's vulnerability and attention. It should communicate competence without sounding clinical or cold, and assistance without implying medical authority. The interface and copy should reduce cognitive load, explain what is happening, and keep the user in control of health data and AI-suggested interpretations.

## Anti-references

MedStory should not look or behave like:

- An AI doctor, symptom checker, diagnostic assistant, or treatment recommendation tool.
- A telemedicine platform, appointment-booking service, insurance portal, or hospital integration hub.
- A complex electronic health record designed for clinicians rather than patients.
- A generic AI chat product where medical history feels like loose conversation instead of a structured record.
- A productivity dashboard that prioritizes metrics, streaks, or gamification over trust and clarity.
- A decorative wellness app that feels soft but does not communicate security, seriousness, and long-term reliability.

## Design Principles

1. Put the user's story before the diagnosis.
Medical diagnoses can change, but the user's symptoms, treatments, outcomes, and lived history remain valuable. The product should organize around the user's health journey rather than forcing everything into rigid diagnostic categories.

2. Reduce work at the moment of capture.
Users should be able to save new information quickly through scan, photo, file, text, or voice-first flows. AI can assist with structuring, but the interface should avoid making users manually maintain complex records.

3. Explain without overstepping.
Medical information should be translated into plain language while preserving the boundary that MedStory organizes and explains information, not diagnoses or recommends care.

4. Keep the user in control.
AI-suggested events, summaries, and explanations should be reviewable, confirmable, editable, and traceable. Privacy choices, exports, deletion, and data handling should be understandable and visible.

5. Design for years, not sessions.
The product becomes more valuable as history accumulates. Timeline browsing, summaries, search, caching, and information architecture should support long-term use across months and years of records.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

MedStory should be usable by non-technical users across different ages, health contexts, and levels of medical literacy. The product should support large text, high contrast, screen-reader labels for actionable controls, minimum 48dp tap targets, readable plain-language explanations, and locale-aware UI and AI outputs.

Because users may be anxious, fatigued, in pain, or preparing for time-sensitive appointments, flows should minimize memory burden, avoid ambiguous labels, and make error recovery clear. Privacy and security controls should be understandable without legal or technical expertise. Motion should respect reduced-motion preferences and should convey state rather than decorate the interface.
